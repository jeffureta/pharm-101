Glycerol Trinitrate (GTN)

Also known as: Nitroglycerin (NTG) • 1,2,3-trinitroxypropane

DRUG CLASS Vasodilator (Organic Nitrate)
PRIMARY MECHANISM NO Release → ↑ cGMP → Relaxation
FIRST-PASS BIOAVAILABILITY 10% – 20% (Low)
ELIMINATION HALF-LIFE 2 – 8 minutes
SUB-LINGUAL ONSET 1 – 3 minutes (Duration: 15–30 min)
KEY PHENOMENON Tachyphylaxis (Need 8h drug-free)
tune

Pharmacodynamics (PD)

Cellular mechanism, vascular selectivity & myocardial workload reduction

1
Denitration in Vascular Smooth Muscle

GTN is bioactivated enzymatically (mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase ALDH2) releasing Nitric Oxide (NO).

2
Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase (sGC) Activation

NO activates sGC, increasing cyclic GMP (cGMP) synthesis from GTP.

3
Protein Kinase G (PKG) Cascade

cGMP stimulates PKG → dephosphorylation of myosin light chains → reduction in intracellular Ca²⁺.

4
Smooth Muscle Relaxation & Vasodilation

Prominent venodilation (low dose) followed by arteriolar dilation (high dose).

water_drop

Low Dose / Concentration

Veins First (Venodilation)
  • Veins are affected first at lower concentrations due to higher enzymatic susceptibility.
  • Large venous capacitance pooling reduces cardiac venous return (preload).
  • Decreases stroke volume without significant fall in systemic blood pressure.
  • Significantly lowers Left Ventricular (LV) end-diastolic volume.
speed

Higher Dose / Concentration

Arterial Dilation
  • Causes systemic arterial & arteriolar relaxation.
  • Reduces Systemic Blood Pressure and afterload, decreasing Cardiac Output (CO) and myocardial workload.
  • Directly dilates large epicardial coronary arteries, relieving coronary spasm and improving collateral flow.
  • Decreases LV wall tension (Laplace: T = P × r / 2h), drastically lowering myocardial oxygen demand.
lightbulb
Key Myocardial Mechanism: GTN decreases LV volume → decreases LV wall tension → subsequent substantial reduction in myocardial oxygen consumption (MVO₂).
Note: GTN also inhibits platelet aggregation, but this is clinically insignificant compared to its hemodynamic effects.

metabolism Metabolism & Excretion

Extensively metabolized by hepatic glutathione-dependent organic nitrate reductase into 1,2-glyceryl dinitrate and 1,3-glyceryl dinitrate. The 1,2-dinitro derivative retains significant vasodilator activity. Inactive metabolites are excreted primarily by the kidneys.

Tachyphylaxis (Nitrate Tolerance)

Key Clinical & Bedside Concept

Cause: Rapid diminution of response with continuous nitrate exposure due to depletion of tissue sulfhydryl (-SH) groups in vascular smooth muscle, accompanied by neurohormonal activation and superoxide production.

High Risk Scenarios: Continuous IV GTN infusions, round-the-clock transdermal patches, or frequent long-acting preparations.

check_circle The Solution: An intentional “Drug-free” interval of 8 hours between doses allows vascular sulfhydryl reserves to regenerate.

Clinical Uses & Indications

Emergency and acute coronary care applications

cardiology

Angina Pectoris

Acute relief and prophylaxis of stable, vasospastic (Prinzmetal), and effort-induced angina. Rapid relief via sublingual spray or tablet.

1st Line Relief
ecg

Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)

Unstable angina and Non-STEMI / STEMI with ongoing ischemic discomfort. Reduces preload, LV wall stress, and increases collateral flow.

Titrated IV / SL
lungs

Acute Pulmonary Oedema (APO)

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema and acute severe heart failure. Aggressive venodilation unloads the pulmonary circuit and reduces capillary wedge pressure.

High-Dose Bolus / Infusion
speed

Hypertensive Emergencies

Severe blood pressure spikes with acute end-organ damage (e.g. hypertensive heart failure, encephalopathy, perioperative hypertension).

Controlled Afterload Reduction
warning_amber

Aortic Dissection (With Beta-Blockade)

Used to lower blood pressure in Type A and Type B acute aortic dissection. CRITICAL RULE: Always co-administer or precede with adequate beta-blockade (e.g., labetalol, esmolol) to suppress reflex tachycardia and aortic shear stress (dP/dt).

Requires Prior Beta-Blockade

Adverse Effects

Direct pharmacological extensions of vascular smooth muscle relaxation

psychology

Headache ("Nitrate Headache")

Most common side effect (~50% of patients). Secondary to cerebral and meningeal arterial dilatation. Usually throbbing; often attenuates with continued therapy or responds to simple analgesia.

airline_seat_recline_extra

Postural / Orthostatic Hypotension

Marked pooling of venous blood in capacitance vessels leads to sudden cerebral hypoperfusion upon standing. Advise patients to sit or lie down before sublingual administration.

favorite

Reflex Tachycardia

Arterial pressure drop triggers sympathetic baroreceptor activation, increasing heart rate. Paradoxically increases myocardial oxygen demand if blood pressure drops excessively.

opacity

Salt and Water Retention

Observed primarily with chronic longer-acting nitrate regimens. Mediated by compensatory renal renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis activation in response to lower renal perfusion pressures.

Precautions & Contraindications

Absolute red flags, preload dependency & fixed output states

trending_down

Hypotension

Baseline systolic blood pressure < 90 mmHg or drop > 30 mmHg below patient baseline. Vasodilation further impairs coronary perfusion pressure (CPP = DBP - LVEDP).

crisis_alert

Inferior / Posterior MI & RV Infarct

Crucial Red Flag! In right ventricular (RV) infarction, the non-compliant RV depends entirely on high right atrial venous preload to maintain cardiac output. GTN venodilation can induce profound, fatal circulatory collapse!

lock

Fixed Cardiac Output States

Severe Aortic Stenosis (AS), Mitral Stenosis, Cardiac Tamponade, or Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM). Afterload reduction without stroke volume compensation leads to catastrophic hypotension.

monitor_heart

Significant Bradycardia or Tachycardia

Heart rate < 50 bpm (impairs stroke compensation) or severe tachycardia > 100 bpm (shortens diastolic coronary filling time).

dangerous

PDE-5 Inhibitor Co-Administration (High-Yield Clinical Pearl)

Absolute contraindication if phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors taken recently (sildenafil / vardenafil within 24 hours; tadalafil within 48 hours). Synergistic cGMP accumulation causes profound, refractory, and potentially fatal cardiovascular shock.
