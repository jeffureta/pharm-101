Furosemide

Also known as: Frusemide • Lasix • Anthranilic acid / Sulfonamide derivative

DRUG CLASS Loop Diuretic (High-Ceiling)
PRIMARY TARGET NKCC2 Cotransporter (Thick Ascending Limb)
ORAL BIOAVAILABILITY ~50% (The 50% Rule: 40 mg PO ≈ 20 mg IV)
PEAK EFFECT 30 min (IV) • 1 hour (PO)
DURATION OF ACTION 2 – 3 hours IV (“Lasts Six” hours PO)
SECONDARY ACTION ↑ COX-2 / Prostaglandins → Venodilation
tune

Pharmacodynamics (PD)

Two distinct mechanisms: NKCC2 cotransporter inhibition & prostaglandin venodilation

1
Luminal Secretion via Organic Acid Transporters

Furosemide travels bound to albumin (95%) and is actively secreted by proximal tubule OAT transporters into tubular fluid to reach its luminal target.

2
NKCC2 Cotransporter Inhibition in Thick Ascending Limb (TAL)

Reversibly binds the chloride site of the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ (NKCC2) cotransporter on the apical membrane, blocking ~25% of total filtered NaCl reabsorption.

3
Abolition of Transepithelial Positive Potential

Inhibition of K⁺ recycling eliminates the lumen-positive electrical gradient (+8 to +10 mV), abolishing the paracellular drive and massively increasing Magnesium (Mg²⁺) and Calcium (Ca²⁺) excretion.

4
COX-2 & Prostaglandin-Mediated Vascular Effects

Stimulates renal COX-2 expression, increasing vasodilatory prostaglandins (PGE₂ and PGI₂). This produces rapid systemic venodilation and increases renal cortical blood flow.

speed

Acute Hemodynamic Venodilation (5–15 min)

Prostaglandin Mediated
  • Occurs before any onset of diuresis via renal prostaglandin (PGE₂) and sympathetic modulation.
  • Promotes venous capacitance pooling, rapidly decreasing cardiac venous return (preload).
  • Significantly lowers Left Ventricular Filling Pressure (LVFP) and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure in acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema (APO).
  • Blunted by concomitant NSAID administration!
water_drop

Renal Natriuretic Diuresis (30–60 min)

High-Ceiling Diuresis
  • High-ceiling natriuresis: Excretes up to 20%–25% of filtered sodium load.
  • Causes profound loss of Na⁺, Cl⁻, K⁺, H⁺, Mg²⁺, and Ca²⁺.
  • Disrupts the hypertonic medullary gradient, impairing the kidney's ability to concentrate urine.
  • Reduces total extracellular fluid (ECF) volume and interstitial edema.
lightbulb
Key Emergency Pearl: In acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema, furosemide provides rapid symptomatic relief within 5–15 minutes through prostaglandin-mediated venodilation and preload reduction well before any urine is produced in the catheter!

directions_alt Active Secretion to Site of Action

Because furosemide is >95% protein-bound to circulating albumin, minimal drug enters the tubular lumen via glomerular filtration. Instead, it reaches its luminal site of action in the thick ascending limb through active secretion via organic acid transporters (OAT1/OAT3) in the proximal convoluted tubule.

Drug Interaction Note: Uremic organic anions, probenecid, and NSAIDs compete for these proximal tubule transporters, reducing luminal delivery and causing furosemide resistance.

metabolism Metabolism & Excretion

Approximately 65% of furosemide is excreted unchanged by the kidneys via tubular secretion and urinary clearance. The remaining ~35% undergoes hepatic glucuronidation (furosemide glucuronide) with negligible cytochrome P450 oxidation. Duration of action is 2–3 hours following IV bolus and 6–8 hours orally (“Lasix” = Lasts Six hours).

Clinical Uses & Indications

Emergency volume overload, hyperkalemia, hypercalcemia, and renal assessment

lungs

Acute Pulmonary Oedema (APO)

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema and acute decompensated heart failure. Dual benefit: rapid venodilation unloads pulmonary capillaries immediately, followed by massive diuresis.

20 – 80 mg IV Bolus
water_damage

Oedematous States & Cirrhosis

Volume overload in hepatic cirrhosis with ascites, nephrotic syndrome, and congestive heart failure. Frequently combined with spironolactone (e.g. 100 mg spironolactone : 40 mg furosemide).

Titrated Oral / IV
vital_signs

Severe Hyperkalaemia

Enhances urinary potassium excretion by delivering high sodium loads to the cortical collecting duct. Most effective when administered alongside isotonic saline rehydration.

40 – 80 mg IV with Saline
bone

Severe Symptomatic Hypercalcaemia

Inhibits the transepithelial potential in the Loop of Henle, promoting marked calciuria. Crucial rule: Administer ONLY after complete volume rehydration with 0.9% normal saline.

Post-Hydration Calciuria
vital_signs

Acute Kidney Injury & Furosemide Stress Test (FST)

Used to evaluate tubular functional reserve in early Stage 1–2 AKI. A standardized high-dose bolus (1.0 mg/kg in loop-naive patients or 1.5 mg/kg in previous loop users) is administered. Urine output < 200 mL over 2 hours predicts progression to severe Stage 3 AKI and need for Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT).

Adverse Effects & Ototoxicity

Electrolyte depletion, acid-base disturbances, and cochlear toxicity

science

Hypokalaemic, Hypochloraemic Metabolic Alkalosis

High Na⁺ delivery to the cortical collecting duct stimulates excessive ENaC reabsorption and reciprocal aldosterone-driven K⁺ and H⁺ secretion. Leads to weakness, cardiac arrhythmias, and metabolic alkalosis.

hearing_disabled

Ototoxicity (Tinnitus & Sensorineural Hearing Loss)

Inhibits the NKCC cotransporter in the stria vascularis of the inner ear, disrupting endolymph electrolyte composition. Dose-related and usually reversible, but heightened with rapid IV bolus administration (> 4 mg/min), renal failure, or concomitant aminoglycosides (gentamicin).

electric_bolt

Hypomagnesaemia & Hypocalcaemia

Loss of the lumen-positive potential in the thick ascending limb abolishes paracellular divalent cation reabsorption. Hypomagnesemia impairs cardiac membrane stability and makes hypokalemia refractory to replacement.

flare

Hyperuricaemia & Precipitated Gout

Volume contraction stimulates proximal tubule uric acid reabsorption, while furosemide directly competes with urate for tubular secretion, triggering acute gout flares.

allergy

Sulfonamide Hypersensitivity & Interstitial Nephritis

Furosemide is a sulfonamide derivative. Potential cross-reactions include skin rashes, eosinophilia, drug fever, and acute allergic tubulointerstitial nephritis.

Precautions & Contraindications

Volume depletion, electrolyte red flags & critical drug interactions

crisis_alert

Severe Hypovolaemia & Pre-Renal Shock

Crucial Red Flag! Administering loop diuretics to a hypovolemic patient converts prerenal azotemia into ischemic acute tubular necrosis (ATN) and triggers profound circulatory collapse. Always verify intravascular volume.

monitor_heart

Severe Hypokalaemia (K⁺ < 3.0 mmol/L)

Diuresis drastically accelerates potassium excretion, precipitating life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias (torsades de pointes, ventricular tachycardia). Correct potassium before aggressive diuresis.

block

Anuria Unresponsive to Test Dose

Established complete anuria unresponsive to adequate trial dosing indicates extensive parenchymal damage or urinary tract obstruction. High doses in anuria offer no benefit and drastically increase ototoxicity risk.

medication

Digoxin & Lithium Co-Administration

High-Risk Toxicity! Furosemide-induced hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia drastically potentiate digoxin myocardial toxicity, while volume depletion decreases renal lithium clearance leading to neurotoxicity.

warning

NSAID & Probenecid Drug Interaction

Concomitant NSAID administration inhibits renal COX-2 prostaglandin synthesis, preventing acute venodilation and blunting natriuresis by up to 50%. Both NSAIDs and probenecid compete with furosemide for proximal tubule OAT secretion.
