Propofol

Also known as: 2,6-diisopropylphenol • "Milk of Amnesia" • 1% Lipid Emulsion

DRUG CLASS IV Anaesthetic / Sedative-Hypnotic
PRIMARY MECHANISM GABAA Potentiation → ↑ Cl⁻ Influx
IV ONSET OF ACTION < 30–45 seconds (1 circulation time)
OFFSET MECHANISM Rapid Redistribution (Brain → Muscle/Fat)
CLINICAL DURATION 3 – 8 minutes (Distribution t½: 2–4 min)
CRITICAL PEARL NO Analgesic Effect (Needs Opioid)
tune

Pharmacodynamics (PD)

Multiple organ system effects: CNS, Cardiovascular & Respiratory

1
GABAA Receptor Allosteric Binding

Propofol binds specifically to transmembrane domains on the β-subunit of the ligand-gated GABAA receptor complex in the central nervous system.

2
Chloride Ion Channel Opening

Significantly increases channel opening duration and potentiates endogenous GABA activity, driving massive intracellular chloride (Cl⁻) influx.

3
Neuronal Hyperpolarization & CNS Depression

Hyperpolarizes post-synaptic neuronal membranes, creating widespread inhibition of cortical and subcortical neurotransmission, leading to rapid hypnosis and amnesia.

psychology

Central Nervous System (CNS)

Hypnotic & Anticonvulsant
  • Profound Hypnosis: Smooth and rapid loss of consciousness within 30–45 seconds.
  • Potent Anticonvulsant: Terminates status epilepticus refractory to benzodiazepines.
  • Decreases ICP: Lowers both Cerebral Metabolic Rate ($CMRO_2$) and Cerebral Blood Flow ($CBF$).
  • NO ANALGESIC EFFECT: Does NOT provide any pain relief. Must always co-administer analgesics (e.g., fentanyl, ketamine) for painful procedures.
favorite

Cardiovascular System

Preload & Afterload Drop
  • Marked Hypotension: Decreases both preload (venodilation) and afterload (arteriolar relaxation).
  • Negative Inotropic Action: Directly suppresses myocardial contractility.
  • Exaggerated in Hypovolemia & Elderly: Profound SBP drops occur in shocked or volume-depleted patients.
  • Blunted Baroreceptors: Inhibits baroreceptor response, so reflex tachycardia is noticeably blunted.

lungs Respiratory & Secondary Effects

Respiratory Depression: Dose-related central depression of respiratory drive. Markedly decreases respiratory rate and blunts ventilatory response to hypercapnia and hypoxia. Causes significant reduction in tidal volume and loss of protective pharyngeal/laryngeal airway reflexes. Transient apnea occurs in > 50% of induction doses.

Anti-Emetic Action: Unique among general anaesthetics, propofol has direct anti-emetic properties even at sub-hypnotic doses ($10–20\text{ mg}$), mediated via dopamine and 5-HT3 pathway modulation in the chemoreceptor trigger zone.

lightbulb
Key Emergency Pearl: Propofol produces rapid, deep hypnosis but has zero analgesic activity. Procedural sedation without appropriate analgesia will result in purposeful patient movement, tachycardia, and hypertension in response to painful stimuli despite deep sedation.

The Cardinal Kinetic Rule: Redistribution vs Metabolism

Why patients wake up in under 10 minutes

Following an intravenous bolus, propofol rapidly equilibrates across the blood-brain barrier into the vessel-rich central nervous system within 30 seconds due to its high lipophilicity.

Waking up is NOT due to rapid hepatic breakdown: Patient awakening after a single dose is driven primarily by rapid redistribution down concentration gradients from the brain into poorly perfused peripheral compartments (skeletal muscle and adipose tissue).

info Context-Sensitive Half-Time (CSHT): With prolonged continuous infusions (> 48h), peripheral tissues become saturated, and awakening becomes dependent on slow metabolic elimination, prolonging emergence time.

metabolism Metabolism & Extra-Hepatic (Pulmonary) Clearance

Propofol undergoes extensive and rapid clearance in the liver via glucuronidation (UGT1A9) and CYP2B6/2C9 hydroxylation to inactive water-soluble quinols. Key Clinical Anomaly: Propofol’s total body clearance ($1.5–2.2\text{ L/min}$) exceeds normal hepatic blood flow ($1.2\text{ L/min}$), providing definitive proof of significant extra-hepatic metabolism in the lungs. Metabolites are excreted renally (< 1% unchanged).

Clinical Uses & Indications

Emergency procedural sedation, RSI induction, and ICU maintenance

timer

Procedural Sedation

Emergency department reductions (dislocated shoulder, fractures), synchronized electrical cardioversion, and endoscopy. Rapid onset and crystal-clear emergence.

0.5 – 1.0 mg/kg Slow Bolus
airline_seat_flat

Adult Rapid Sequence Induction (RSI)

Standard induction agent for endotracheal intubation in hemodynamically stable patients. Rapidly abolishes laryngeal and pharyngeal reflexes.

1.0 – 2.5 mg/kg IV
child_care

Pediatric Anaesthesia Induction

Higher doses are required in infants and children due to larger volume of distribution ($V_d$) and elevated weight-normalized clearance rates.

2.5 – 3.5 mg/kg IV
monitor_heart

Maintenance of Anaesthesia / TIVA

Total Intravenous Anaesthesia (TIVA) using target-controlled infusions (TCI) or continuous infusion pumps for surgical operations and ventilator tolerance.

4 – 12 mg/kg/h Titrated
elderly

Elderly & Shock Dosing Adjustment

In patients over 65 years or those with volume depletion, reduce induction doses by 50%–70% ($0.5–1.0\text{ mg/kg}$ slowly) to prevent sudden profound cardiovascular collapse. Always optimize intravascular volume first.

Adverse Effects & PRIS

Direct pharmacological consequences and high-risk infusion syndromes

trending_down

Systemic Hypotension

Direct myocardial depression combined with arterial and venous vasodilation. SBP drops by 20%–40% in standard inductions, significantly intensified by baseline dehydration or elderly age.

air

Apnea & Airway Obstruction

Abolition of upper airway tone and profound depression of the medullary respiratory center. Risk of severe hypoxia and pulmonary aspiration if airway is unprotected.

front_hand

Severe Pain on Injection

Reported in 30%–70% of peripheral injections due to irritation by the aqueous phase lipid emulsion. Mitigated by using larger antecubital veins, co-administering IV lidocaine ($1\text{ mg/kg}$), or cold saline flush.

warning

Propofol Infusion Syndrome (PRIS)

A potentially fatal mitochondrial toxic syndrome seen with prolonged (> 48h) or high-dose (> $4–5\text{ mg/kg/h}$) infusions. Characterized by refractory metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalemia, hepatomegaly, acute renal failure, and refractory bradyarrhythmias / cardiac arrest.

Precautions & Contraindications

Severe red flags, hemodynamic collapse & unmonitored airways

crisis_alert

Severe Hemodynamic Instability / Shock

Crucial Red Flag! In uncorrected hypovolemic, septic, or cardiogenic shock, sympathetic tone is the only factor maintaining organ perfusion. Propofol-induced sympatholysis and direct venodilation precipitate rapid cardiac arrest.

air

Unmonitored / Insecure Airway

Propofol must NEVER be administered without immediate access to advanced airway equipment (bag-valve-mask, suction, endotracheal intubation, capnography) and personnel certified in airway rescue.

lock

Fixed Cardiac Output States

Severe Aortic Stenosis, severe Mitral Stenosis, or Obstructive Cardiomyopathy. Sudden afterload collapse without compensatory stroke volume augmentation causes severe coronary hypoperfusion.

egg

Egg & Soybean Allergy Cross-Reactivity

Formulation contains 10% soybean oil and 1.2% purified egg phospholipid (lecithin). Absolute contraindication in patients with severe true anaphylaxis to egg or soy products (note: most egg allergies are to egg white albumin, not yolk lecithin, but caution is mandatory).

warning

Elderly & Frail Patients (Marked Dose Sensitivity)

Volume of distribution is reduced and central nervous system sensitivity is heightened. Reduce induction dose by 50%–70% and inject slowly over 60 seconds to avoid dramatic hypotensive precipitancy.
